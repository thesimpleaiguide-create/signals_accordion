Your body speaks first, often before your conscious mind has caught up.

  • • Heart rate increases: you can feel your pulse in your chest or neck
  • • Face or ears feel 'hot': blood rushes to the surface
  • • Breathing becomes shallow: short, rapid breaths high in the chest
  • • Jaw clenches or fists tighten: your muscles prepare for action
These are not signs of weakness. They are your nervous system doing exactly what it's designed to do. The skill is catching them before they catch you.

While your body braces, your thinking shifts too. These changes are just as important to notice.

  • • Tunnel vision: you stop hearing what the patient is actually saying and start rehearsing your response
  • • Sudden urge to interrupt: you feel compelled to correct, defend and to have the last word
  • • Internal monologue shifts: you stop thinking 'How can I help?' and start thinking 'How dare they?'
When you notice these shifts, pay attention. You are standing at the edge of your window of tolerance and what you do in the next few seconds determines which side of it you stay on.
